# visual-metaphor

The skill that makes an essay watchable. A script about identity and time has no
footage: there is nothing to film, and searching for something to film produces
the exact filler — abstract stock loops, neon circuit boards, humanoid robots —
that makes a video feel unserious.

**The metaphor must carry the argument, not decorate it.** Test: if the visual
changes, does the claim change? If not, it is decoration, and it should be
replaced or removed.

## Method

1. **Name the claim.** One sentence. "A duplicate can hold every attribute and
   still not be the one."
2. **Find what the claim does, not what it is about.** The claim *separates two
   things the viewer assumed were one*. That is a visual operation.
3. **Pick the operation:**
   | Claim shape | Operation |
   |---|---|
   | X and Y are not the same | convergence, then a residue that will not merge |
   | X changed over time | a timeline that lights, or shifts colour |
   | X is being measured wrongly | a plausible UI that gets struck out |
   | X is one instance of many | a zoom that narrows and does not stop |
   | X is a list, Y is a history | a table that disassembles into a network |
   | X is unverifiable | an instrument that returns a number, then fails |
4. **Strip the sci-fi.** Editorial, restrained, typographic. The moment it looks
   like a film about AI, it stops being about the argument.
5. **Leave negative space** for Korean captions — the lower third is spoken for.

## The colour argument

Run two palettes and never mix them inside a beat:

- **Warm** — history, the particular, the church, the person. What the essay is
  moving *toward*.
- **Cold** — UI, scoring, machine-reading, checklists. What the essay is moving
  *away from*.

Then the palette itself argues. A warm element inside a cold panel is an
authored event: the first crack in a machine-shaped inference. A stat sheet that
disassembles from cold into a warm network *is* the thesis, before a word of it
is spoken.

## Worked patterns

**Non-substitutability** — two figures, visibly different, converging attribute
by attribute until identical. A cold `100% MATCH` readout stays on screen while
the narration says something still feels wrong. The contradiction between the
readout and the voice is the argument; do not resolve it by removing either.

**Particularity** — one uninterrupted zoom, cosmos → world → region → settlement
→ one figure. One continuous motion, not five cuts: the claim is about a single
movement. Never render the face.

**Attribution vs. attributes** — a game-style stat sheet that assembles, then
physically disassembles into an event network. The numbers do not survive the
transformation. Not a fade: a fade says the scores were *wrong*, disassembly
says they were *the wrong kind of question*.

**Epistemic limits** — a diagnostic UI that is plausible, returns a confident
number, and is struck out. Make the UI look like something that shipped. The
joke only works if it looks real for a moment.

**Memory vs. participation** — `기억하는 것 ≠ 그 사건의 당사자였던 것`, with the ≠
drawn last, as a stroke. The stroke is the beat.

## Anti-patterns

- Stock B-roll standing in for an idea
- Humanoid robots for anything about AI
- Neon, glitch, cyberpunk, circuit boards
- Rendering the face of a religious figure
- Grotesque or sensational religious imagery
- A graphic that restates the caption in different letters
- Decoration that changes nothing if removed

## Output

Per concept: the claim, the operation, the visual description, the palette
assignment, the keyframes, and what must **not** appear. Write it into the
storyboard's `visual` field so `remotion-video` builds from one source.
