Inspected: `smartlead-prewarmed-burn.mp4` (1440x1080, 30fps, 7.06s) as an 8-frame
1fps grid plus the bundled 3-frame contact sheet.

## Composition

A near-white ground (~#f2f3f5) with a faint horizontal rule system, and every
object on it is a real product surface — flat white cards, thin grey dividers,
soft realistic drop shadows, no ornament. Three account cards sit in a shallow
overlapping row at the left, the campaign editor is a wider card to the right,
and a thin grey connector line physically joins the editor to the pool. Small
uppercase grey captions sit under a group and name it ("PRE-WARMED MAILBOX
POOL", "12 ACCOUNTS") — structural labels, never narration.

Scale is the striking part. The sender-health number ("98", "42") is set far
larger than anything else in the card and carries the whole story at a glance;
the surrounding rows stay small and are texture, not content. Two or three
objects are on screen at once at most. The frame is never busy.

## Camera

Hold on the pool → travel left to the editor → the cursor presses "Launch
campaign" → travel back right along the connector to the same three cards →
final pull-back showing the whole chain small. Movement is lateral and slow,
always along the connector, and every move is motivated by the causality it is
tracing. Action completes during the holds, never mid-travel. The camera never
orbits, never floats, and never zooms for effect.

## Identity

Smartlead's own palette used exactly as the product uses it: purple mark, navy
"Launch campaign" button, green health bars, red at-risk bars, a yellow inline
text highlight in the editor. Nothing is recoloured or restyled for the video.
No glow, bloom, luminous edge or gradient anywhere — depth comes only from soft
neutral shadows under physical-feeling cards.

## Research match

Two rejected drafts taught me what this mechanism actually is. It is not
"authentic UI on a ground" and it is not "a cursor somewhere in frame" — it
is: an actor acts, an authentic surface visibly answers, and the camera
travels along that causality. The reference proves it by revisiting the same
three account cards after the click and showing 98/97/96 become 42/39/38.

So this build has an actor acting twice, and both answers are the product's
own. First the cursor picks the Editor token on ENG.FLOOR in the real office
and the product's own camera flies into the room and opens its own detail
card — recorded, not staged. Then the cursor presses the certificate page's
own footer link 「원본 JSON으로 직접 검증 →」 and the browser replaces the page
with the raw signed response. Navigation and camera state change are both
consequences the product produces; nothing is annotated on top.

Scale discipline is inherited literally. The reference's story rides on one
huge legible value per hold, so here the office is recorded at full phone
size, the certificate is framed on its card so it fills the frame rather than
floating in the page's white margin, and the response is captured at reading
size — a block taller than the frame that the camera travels down, so the
verdict, the content hash and the signature each pass through centre at a
size that survives a phone.

The one deliberate departure is ground tone: the reference is light, this is
charcoal, chosen because the product's authenticated surface is dark. It
shows only in the final URL bar; the rest of the clip is edge-to-edge product.

## Anti-pattern

What the reference proves is avoidable, and what I therefore will not do: no
static centred object with rows appearing under a slow zoom (the reference earns
its motion by revisiting the same cards in a changed state, so my certificate
must arrive as a consequence of the office frame, not merely be zoomed into); no
decorative floating cards, receipts, stamps or drawn certificates — the only
certificate on screen is the product's real page; no invented metrics, agent
names or product states; no narration text repeating what the UI already says;
no neon, glow, gradient wash or glassmorphism; and no busy grid of small cards
where two large legible objects would carry the beat.
