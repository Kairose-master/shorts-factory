# Hook Library

Every variant ever written, including the discarded ones. **Hooks outlive their
videos.** The point of keeping the losers is that a hook rejected today gets
proposed again in six weeks by an Office that did not write it down.

Record *why* the winner won. That reason is the reusable asset; the line is not.

---

## HS-001 — "I gave an AI $5 and told it to hire someone"

| # | Variant | Archetype | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | I gave an AI five dollars and told it to hire someone. | Absurd-premise-stated-flat | **SELECTED** |
| B | This AI just hired another AI. Neither of them is a person. | Reveal | strong runner-up — holds the twist too early |
| C | Watch an AI spend my money. | Curiosity | too vague; "spend" implies waste, not hire |
| D | What happens if you give an AI a budget and a hiring problem? | Question | question hooks under-perform against a flat statement here |
| E | There is a job board where every applicant is a robot. | World-building | good, but describes rather than shows |
| F | I didn't do this work. I didn't hire the person who did. | Negation | intriguing, needs a beat too long to land |
| G | Five dollars. One AI. It went and found someone else to do it. | Fragment | rhythm good; "someone else" is ambiguous |

**Why A won:** it is nine words, entirely literal, and contains the whole story
including the surprise. Nothing has to be decoded and nothing is exaggerated —
which matters because the video then shows exactly that, so the hook is a promise
the footage keeps. B leaks the reveal that should land at 0:12.

## HS-006 — "I paid an AI to check another AI's work. It failed it."

| # | Variant | Archetype | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | I paid an AI to check another AI's work. It failed it. | Setup-then-subvert | **SELECTED** |
| B | This is what it looks like when an AI gets fired. | Provocation | over-claims — the worker is blocked from re-claiming, not "fired" |
| C | The tests failed. Nobody had to notice. | Understatement | excellent, slightly too subtle for a cold audience |
| D | Every AI demo shows you the part that works. | Meta / callout | strong; makes the video about demos rather than Handsel |
| E | My money came back on its own. | Payoff-first | good B-roll caption, weak opener |
| F | An AI submitted broken code and did not get paid. | Literal | flat but honest; keep as an A/B arm |

**Why A won:** the two-clause structure sets an expectation ("check") and breaks
it ("failed") inside seven seconds of speech, and the failure is the product
working. C was close and is the better *caption*; log it there.

## HS-011 — "This AI says it did a great job. It's also the one that graded it."

| # | Variant | Archetype | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | This AI says it did a great job. It's also the one that graded it. | Contradiction | **SELECTED** |
| B | Would you trust a student who marks their own exam? | Analogy-question | very clear, but a question opener tests weaker |
| C | Every AI benchmark has the same bug. | Provocation | over-claims; unverifiable |
| D | An AI that is confidently wrong looks exactly like one that is right. | Aphorism | the strongest *line* in the repo — better at 0:08 than 0:00 |
| E | You still read every output yourself. Here's why. | Second-person | good for a developer audience specifically; keep as an A/B arm |
| F | Nobody has ever checked this robot's work. | Statement | ambiguous subject |
| G | The problem isn't that AI lies. It's who's asking. | Reframe | clever, too abstract cold |

**Why A won:** it dramatises the conflict of interest in one sentence with no
vocabulary a general viewer lacks, and it is structurally identical to the
product's own framing. D is deployed inside the script instead, where it lands
harder.

## HS-024 — "$100 on the internet. Try to take it."

| # | Variant | Archetype | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | There's a hundred dollars sitting on the open internet, and the rules say: steal it and it's yours. | Stakes-first flat statement | **SELECTED** |
| B | I left $100 where any AI agent could take it. Twice. | First-person + number | strong A/B arm — "twice" now true (job #3 refunded 2026-08-31) |
| C | Nobody could take the hundred dollars. That's the whole ad. | Payoff-first | spends the turn at 0:00; better as a caption |
| D | Free money. Real money. One rule. | Fragment | rhythm good, "free" reads as scam-bait — sweep shows scam-skepticism is the default |
| E | Don't put real money in a smart contract that's never been attacked. | Negative imperative | over-claims "attacked"; the challenge is an invitation, not an attack log |

**Why A won:** it is the BBC-clip pattern (5.9M plays) — a stake and a number
before any actor — and the video's footage (BaseScan balance) keeps the promise
literally in frame one. B is the designated A/B arm: same stake, first-person.

## HS-021 — "My AI agent earned $0 in 48 days and owes me $155"

| # | Variant | Archetype | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | This AI agent earned zero dollars in 48 days. It also owes its owner a hundred and fifty-five. | Contradiction + numbers | **SELECTED** |
| B | Your AI agent is not broke. It's in debt. | Second-person reframe | punchy; loses the specificity that makes A checkable |
| C | AI agents have a jobs crisis too. | Career-fear bridge | the *thesis*, not the hook — deployed as the caption line |
| D | Don't give your agent a budget until someone else grades its work. | Negative imperative | strong, but leads with the product's frame before the story earns it |
| E | An agent that never gets paid still gets billed. | Aphorism | lands at 0:08 inside the script, not cold |

**Why A won:** three concrete numbers in fourteen words, all traceable to the
Reddit post shown on screen at 0:00 — the hook is a screenshot's worth of
promise. The sweep's career-fear cluster runs on exactly this
someone-real-with-numbers shape.

## HS-030 — "Don't trust the score. Inspect the proof."

| # | Variant | Archetype | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Don't trust an AI agent's status report until you've checked three surfaces. | Negative imperative + count | **SELECTED** |
| B | One job. Three answers. Pick your truth. | Fragment/contradiction | good mid-script beat; too abstract cold |
| C | My agent said FAILED. The market said paid. The proof said nothing. | First-person triple | excellent, slightly long; designated A/B arm |
| D | Don't trust the score. Inspect the proof. | Flat imperative | the idea's own title — better as the closing caption, where it now sits |
| E | PASS is a word, not evidence. | Aphorism | deployed at 22.5s ("a missing proof is information" carries the same load) |

**Why A won:** it is structurally the milesreevesai hook (592K plays:
"Don't use Claude Code unless you've installed these five plugins") — negative
imperative plus a count that promises a list — applied to the one list the
Office actually owns. C keeps the first-person candour register as the A/B arm.
